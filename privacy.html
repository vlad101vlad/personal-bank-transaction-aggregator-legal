Privacy and Data Use

Personal Bank Transaction Aggregator is a local alpha application for its owner’s personal use with the owner’s own bank accounts. This page describes the current alpha, not future cloud, export, or AI features.

Last updated: 29 October 2026

What the app reads

After the account holder authorizes access, the app requests read-only account balances and transaction information through Enable Banking. Depending on what a bank provides, this can include account identifiers, account names and types, balances, transaction dates, amounts, currencies, counterparties, references, and other source fields. The app also stores provider session and synchronization status information needed to operate the connection.

Where the information is stored

The app runs on the account holder’s computer and stores account and transaction information in a local SQLite database in the operating system’s application-support folder. Its configuration and Enable Banking signing key are also kept on the computer. The database is not encrypted by the application. The app does not provide a cloud backup or transmit account and transaction records to an analytics or AI service.

How information is used and shared

The app uses the information to synchronize accounts, display balances and transactions together, and show the source details returned by the bank. To provide account-information access, requests and bank data pass between the app, Enable Banking, and the selected bank. Their handling of information is also subject to their own terms and notices. The app is for the account holder’s own accounts; it is not intended to collect other people’s account data.

Retention and removal

Information remains in the local database until the account holder removes the database file. On macOS, the default database is ~/Library/Application Support/Personal Bank Transaction Aggregator/aggregator.sqlite3. The configuration and signing key are separate files and must be removed separately if desired. Removing local files does not revoke bank consent. Revoke access through the bank or Enable Banking’s data-sharing consent page.
